    Most AI that people turn to for support is an open conversation with no shape and no end, its memory held inside someone else's product. This is the other kind: a daily practice, bounded, and the person's days are theirs.

    ABOUT

    This was made by Matthew Bushell, a therapeutic coach and Emotional Fitness practitioner with 5th Place, who lives with bipolar and serves on the Lived Experience Council of the International Society for Bipolar Disorders, with David Pinto, who created the beach it stands on, and with Claude. It grew from lived experience, from Emotional Fitness, and from Presence in Action, Louie Gardiner's practice.

    WHY IT EXISTS

    More and more people talk to AI about how they are. Those conversations are open-ended: no shape, no end, and a memory kept inside the company's product. They can turn a person in on themselves: more analysing, more self-watching, less living. This is made the other way, around a practice. Each day you say where you are; it gives your day back in your own words and turns you back to today. Done day after day, this can grow something. A clearer sense of what actually happened, what you felt, and the story you are telling about it. The steadiness to notice without being swept along. Over weeks, your days showing you where you tend to come to rest, and where you might step next. That is the hope in it: not to be fixed, but to be more present in your own life, and more free to choose. Nothing here can promise that; it is a practice, and the practice is yours. It is bounded: each day has a limit, and when it's reached, it rests until tomorrow. And your days are yours: a plain record under your name in an open system, not locked inside any one company, so you can read them, leave, or bring them to another AI.

    HOW IT HOLDS YOU

    It reflects. It doesn't advise, interpret, diagnose or praise. Those are the instructions Claude is given here; they guide it but can't guarantee it, so you remain the judge of what's true for you. It isn't a friend or a therapist, and it won't pretend to be.

    WHERE YOUR WORDS GO

    When you speak, your browser turns your voice into text; Chrome may send it to Google to do that. If you want your day given back, your words go to Claude, made by Anthropic: here on this page, or in your own Claude if you keep your days in a chat. Without Claude, your day is kept just as you said it. Days you keep are written to the beach, an open system created by David Pinto, under your name. They are public: anyone who knows your name can read them. Only your word can change them, and forget me removes them.
