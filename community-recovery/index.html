Meet the LERO

What it does

  • A front door in your members’ own words

    Your LERO’s home page is written by its people, not about them, and it changes as they do.

  • A welcome anyone can use

    From hello to ‘you’re in’ on a phone, with no technical knowledge needed. What should stay on paper, stays on paper.

  • Your constitution, shaped together

    Members read it clause by clause and say yes, suggest a change, or say they’re not sure. Decisions are made in the open.

  • The standards, in your own words

    Read your LERO against CLERO’s 33 national standards, at your own pace, without anyone monitoring you.

  • Recovery capital, privately held

    Each person can see their own recovery grow, gently and privately. It belongs to them.

  • Your journey over time

    What your LERO is aiming for this week, this season and this year, with everyone able to feed back.

  • On the map

    Your LERO’s place on the earth, beside the people and places that help recovery, and other LEROs walking the same road.

  • Growth you can see

    The community’s value made visible as it grows, so its worth is seen over years, not snapshots.

Who it’s for

  • For LEROs — proof that comes from members, not from the organisation’s report about itself.
  • For CLERO — a national picture of LEROs against the 33 standards, held by nobody, monitoring no one.
  • For public bodies — evidence of value that grows over years, in members’ own words.

Lived experience recovery organisations have grown across the country, built by people in recovery for people in recovery. Some are twenty years established; many are just beginning. Yet there’s still no consistent understanding of what a LERO is, or how its worth can be seen.

Across the north and the Midlands, established LEROs are already leading the way, beacons for the movement: among them Sheffield Recovery Community, whose chair sits on CLERO’s board; the long-standing LEROs of West Yorkshire; Chase Recovery; and DATUS in Birmingham, twenty years established.

They show what a LERO becomes when it is led by its members and given time.

The LERO Capacity Building Project, funded by the Office for Health Improvement and Disparities, supports the development and sustainability of LEROs across England. It includes a mentorship programme delivered by Imroc, where LERO leaders mentor one another, and a fully funded LERO Leadership Programme with an accredited Level 3 qualification. CLERO’s 33 national standards set out what a good LERO looks like.

We’d welcome CLERO, and the LEROs leading the way, to take this up, test it, and shape it as a toolkit for the national network. CLERO’s standards say implementation toolkits will follow; this could become one, a living way of meeting the standards.

This is a live proposal, not a finished design. It’s being tested with a LERO in the making, against real decisions with real members, before it’s relied on further.

Questions, challenges, a LERO that wants to talk: all welcome. This work goes by conversation, and anything complex gets time to reflect and a proper answer.
